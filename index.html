Topic 5.2: Additive Synthesis & Fourier Principle

Additive synthesis builds a sound by adding simple tones together. Instead of starting with a complex sound and filtering it, additive synthesis builds the sound up from the partials (many sine tones).

Listening to: Pure fundamental sine tone.

Macro: Fundamental Only (Pure Sine)

The purity of the sine tone, similar to a tuning fork. The mathematical principle of Fourier states that any repeating sound can be made from adding these sine waves.

Analogy: a row of tuning forks. Strike several tuning forks at once. Each one rings at its own pitch and strength. Change how loud each fork is and you change the color of the chord.
Why this matters for MIDI: Additive instruments give you controls like brightness and formant as single knobs. A single controller, such as the mod wheel, can change dozens of partials at once, instead of programming each one by hand.
